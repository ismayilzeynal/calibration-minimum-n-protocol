Line chart of split-half reliability against number of rated answers per student, 5 to 200, for four calibration indices in sample 1. Bias and Brier curves rise steeply and cross 0.70 near 15 and 21 answers; gamma and AUROC curves stay far lower, with AUROC crossing 0.70 only near 173 answers and gamma never crossing within 200 answers. A vertical dashed line at 15 answers marks the median reporter and is labelled at the top left of the plot.

## Figure 2 (fig2_undefined_temporal.png, byte-identical copy)

Two panels. Panel a: percentage of students whose gamma, or AUROC under the plan rule, cannot be computed on the full answer set falls from 48 percent at 5 answers to 7 percent at 200; under the tie convention AUROC is undefined for 31 and 4 percent, and the shaded gap between the lines is the students who gave every answer an identical rating, for whom AUROC equals 0.50. Panel b: bias reliability under temporal halves is consistently below random halves, 0.70 versus 0.75 at 20 answers and 0.89 versus 0.97 at 200.

Facts used: gamma undefined 48.2% (N = 5) and 6.8% (N = 200); tie-convention AUROC undefined 31.4% and 4.1%; bias random .754 and temporal .698 at N = 20, .970 and .890 at N = 200; temporal below random at all 11 grid points.
